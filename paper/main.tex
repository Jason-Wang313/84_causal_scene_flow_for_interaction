\begin{abstract}
Interactive robot perception should not treat all 3D scene flow as equivalent. A robot pushing an object needs to distinguish motion caused by its action from passive conveyor flow, exogenous distractor motion, camera/ego motion, occlusion artifacts, and articulated coupling. We rebuilt this paper around a deterministic local interaction-flow benchmark with five physical-shift splits, eight methods, seven seeds, 11,760 main rollouts, 2,058 ablation rollouts, and 25,200 stress rollouts. The benchmark is useful, but the result is negative. On the combined hard-shift split, the proposed causal scene-flow model reaches 0.7609 mask F1 and 0.5646 downstream target success. A learned correlation classifier reaches 0.7723 mask F1 and 0.5476 target success. The proposed model is safer, with lower unsafe contact and slightly lower passive false attribution, but the paired mask-F1 difference versus the learned baseline is $-0.0114 \pm 0.0184$. Worse, the correlation-only ablation improves to 0.7878 mask F1. We therefore mark this paper \textbf{KILL/ARCHIVE}: the benchmark is reproducible and informative, but the proposed mechanism is not supported well enough for an ICLR-main-target submission.
\end{abstract}

\section{Research Question}
The intended claim was:
\begin{quote}
Infer which scene-flow components are caused by robot action versus passive motion.
\end{quote}
This is a real robotics problem. Passive flow and action-caused flow can both be large, aligned, or spatially adjacent. A manipulation policy that attributes passive motion to its own action may choose the wrong target, collide with a distractor, or overestimate controllability. A positive paper would need to show that causal decomposition changes perception in a way that improves both mask attribution and downstream interaction.

\section{Benchmark}
The v4 benchmark simulates local 3D interaction scenes with point-level flow masks. Each scene has an action vector, causal object motion, passive flow, distractor contact flow, articulated coupling, occlusion, ego motion, and sensor noise. The five splits are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{clean contact:} direct robot-caused motion with mild passive flow;
\item \textbf{passive conveyor:} strong passive motion unrelated to the robot;
\item \textbf{articulated coupling:} delayed or attenuated action-caused motion through linked parts;
\item \textbf{occluded interaction:} partially hidden robot-caused flow;
\item \textbf{combined hard shift:} passive flow, articulation, occlusion, and distractor contact combined.
\end{itemize}
Metrics include causal mask F1, precision, recall, occluded recall, passive false attribution, action-effect vector error, downstream target success, unsafe contact, Brier score, and calibration error.

\section{Methods}
We compare:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Flow magnitude threshold:} labels large motion as causal.
\item \textbf{Action-direction projection:} labels flow aligned with the robot action.
\item \textbf{Rigid scene-flow cluster:} groups coherent moving points.
\item \textbf{Temporal difference mask:} subtracts scene-level median flow.
\item \textbf{Noncausal flow-transformer proxy:} uses contact and flow features without intervention structure.
\item \textbf{Learned correlation classifier:} predicts causal masks from action-flow correlation and contact features.
\item \textbf{Causal scene-flow model:} subtracts passive flow, uses action conditioning, articulation priors, and occlusion reasoning.
\item \textbf{Oracle causal mask:} hidden ground-truth ceiling.
\end{itemize}

\section{Why This Is Not Submission-Ready}
The project fails the ICLR-main readiness gate for three reasons.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Primary metric failure.} The proposed model loses to the learned correlation classifier on combined hard-shift mask F1.
\item \textbf{Ablation contradiction.} The correlation-only causal head improves over the full structured causal decomposition.
\item \textbf{Evidence ceiling.} The benchmark is deterministic and reproducible, but it is local and synthetic, not hardware evidence, not a high-fidelity simulator benchmark, and not an accepted external dataset.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
Causal source separation in scene flow remains important for interaction. The v4 benchmark shows that naive flow methods badly over-attribute passive motion, and explicit causal modeling can reduce unsafe contacts. But the proposed mechanism does not beat a learned correlation classifier on mask attribution and fails a central ablation gate. The honest terminal action is \textbf{KILL/ARCHIVE}.
